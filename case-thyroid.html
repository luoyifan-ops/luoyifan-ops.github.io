Multi-Omic Characterization of Thyroid Cancer Molecular Subtypes

Bulk and single-cell transcriptomic analysis of BRAF/RAS-driven tumors
Type Course project, BIOINF 545 Institution University of Michigan Timeline Jan – Apr 2025
Background

Papillary thyroid cancers are usually classified by their driver mutation — BRAF or RAS — but a meaningful subset of tumors carry neither. These "non-BRAF/RAS" tumors are harder to place: are they a genuinely distinct molecular entity, or do they simply lack a detectable driver while still behaving like one of the two known subtypes? The question matters for how they'd eventually be risk-stratified.

Data & Methods
  • Analyzed bulk RNA-seq and SNP data from 488 TCGA-THCA tumor samples to characterize transcriptional differences between BRAF-mutant, RAS-mutant, and non-BRAF/RAS tumors.
  • Ran differential expression analysis with PyDESeq2, identifying 968 BRAF-enriched and 429 RAS-enriched genes (log2FC > 2.5, p < 0.05).
  • Conducted Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (clusterProfiler, R), finding immune-response enrichment in BRAF tumors and mitochondrial / metal-ion stress pathways in RAS tumors.
  • Applied PCA and UMAP dimensionality reduction, then k-nearest-neighbor classification and neighborhood enrichment scoring to locate non-BRAF/RAS tumors relative to the two canonical subtypes.
  • Scored BRAF/RAS gene signatures across every tumor sample using single-sample GSEA (ssGSEA).
  • Validated the bulk-derived signatures in an independent single-cell cohort (GSE193581, 63,322 cells): QC filtering, normalization, and Harmony batch correction in Scanpy; subset to 9,667 malignant cells; cell-type annotation and signature scoring.
  • Trained a logistic regression model on the mutation profiles of non-BRAF/RAS samples to test whether co-occurring mutations (e.g. EIF1AX, CACNA1B) associate with BRAF- or RAS-like transcriptional phenotypes.
Findings

Across both the bulk-tumor cohort and the independent single-cell validation set, non-BRAF/RAS tumors consistently clustered transcriptionally with BRAF-driven tumors — despite the absence of the canonical driver mutation itself.

Absence of a driver mutation doesn't mean absence of the driver's transcriptional program. The signature persisted even after moving from bulk tissue to single malignant cells, which rules out the simplest alternative explanation — that the bulk signal was just diluted contamination from surrounding BRAF-mutant tissue.
Implications

Molecular subtype — not just driver-mutation status on its own — may be the more clinically meaningful way to characterize these tumors. That has downstream relevance for how "driver-negative" thyroid cancers might eventually be risk-stratified or considered for BRAF-pathway-adjacent treatment approaches.
